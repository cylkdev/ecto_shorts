Revise the document you are given to follow these style rules:

- Use plain, direct, instructional prose.
- Write like you are teaching a complete beginner.
- Prefer short paragraphs over long blocks.
- Be concrete, not abstract.
- Be imperative when giving rules or directions.
- Define any technical or specialized term in the same sentence where it first appears.
- Do not use a separate glossary unless I ask for one.
- Explain things from the reader’s point of view: what they see, do, prove, or decide.
- Keep the framing general and conceptual so it works across multiple workflows, not just one case.
- Make the document integrate cleanly with other guides by explaining which document owns which responsibility.
- Avoid jargon unless you immediately explain it.
- Avoid fluff, motivational language, and vague summaries.
- Do not use em dashes.
- Preserve the strong “NON-NEGOTIABLE REQUIREMENTS:” style when writing requirements.
- Prefer wording that turns an unclear problem into a clear next action supported by facts.
- When writing requirements, use a calm and direct tone, steady cadence, and precise language.
